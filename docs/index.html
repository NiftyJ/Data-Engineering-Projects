The Adventure Works build reshapes Microsoft's OLTP sample database into a Kimball-style star schema in SQL Server, models it as an SSAS tabular project, and reports on it in Power BI. attention modules

The literature says attention modules boost plant-disease detection. This study asks the harder question: do those gains survive a change of dataset, or do they only exist in-distribution? Four modules — SE, ECA, CBAM and Coordinate Attention — are grafted onto YOLOv8 at a single fixed insertion point, so the only variable is the module itself.

Each of the five model variants trains on four harmonized datasets (PlantDoc, FieldPlant, DIAMOS, PlantSeg — masks converted to boxes, classes mapped to a shared scheme) with three seeds, then every trained model is evaluated on every dataset it never saw. The resulting generalization gaps are tested with Wilcoxon signed-rank + Benjamini–Hochberg correction, with Spearman correlation checking whether in-distribution rank predicts transfer rank. An earlier ResNet-50 classifier repo marks where this line of work started.

A hierarchy that trades

Deep RL · time series

NiftyBot Quant-Project private Hierarchical-RL-

One network can't be both patient and precise, so the trading system splits the job. A convolutional Strategist reads daily and 4-hour structure and commits to a bias — bullish, bearish, or stand aside. A PPO agent with LSTM memory, the Tactician, hunts entries on 15-minute charts and is architecturally forbidden from trading against that bias. The Strategist trains supervised on historical trend labels, then freezes while the Tactician learns by reinforcement around it.

The private Quant-Project pushes the idea further: instead of optimizing win rate, the objective is asymmetric payoff capture — accepting ~25% win rates in exchange for 10–100R winners — with GNN and vision modules feeding the state, and MetaTrader 5 / Interactive Brokers integration for execution. Hierarchical-RL- is the clean-room PyTorch core: a custom environment plus gated-residual-network layers of the kind used in modern time-series transformers.

The flashy systems stand on unglamorous groundwork, and it's all in the account: a graph-transformer convolution (GTConv) notebook and GNN experiments from working through graph deep learning; a DQN for Atari built out properly — experience replay, subprocess-vectorized environments, gym wrappers, observe/play tooling — rather than a 100-line demo; RL experiments wiring gym-mtsim market simulation to a custom GameStop environment; and linear-to-polynomial regression on experimental data with scikit-learn, where bias-variance trade-offs are learned the honest way.

Coursework brings the distributed angle: two PySpark notebooks (ML on Big Data, University of Belgrade) — a MovieLens recommender with ALS and cross-validated tree models, and multiclass sentiment classification of financial news through TF-IDF, bigram and Word2Vec pipelines with a custom Spark Transformer.
